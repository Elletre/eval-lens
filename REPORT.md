Generated by `eval-lens report` from the runs in `data/`. The descriptive sections come from
the queries in `sql/`, each with a twin in `src/eval_lens/metrics.py` that a test holds equal
to it; the two experiments at the end are computed in Python alone.

Fixture runs never enter a comparison. 2 A/A pairs are too: two runs of one variant with nothing between them, which is the control rather than a comparison.

## 1. Which cases carry information

A case is **informative** when its pass rate differs between the two sides of a comparison. **Saturated** and **floored** cases never differ because they always pass or always fail; **quiet** ones move between runs but never between the sides of a comparison, which is dispersion without an answer.

The largest of these, 14%, is the floor under any difference these suites report: an effect smaller than that cannot be told from the instrument shifting.

## 4. What the suite says when nothing changed

One variant was run twice under identical settings, and the pair was then scored exactly as a real comparison is scored. Everything it reports is the instrument: the right answer is no difference and no informative case.

| Pair | Cases | Cases that moved | Δ pass rate | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lab: baseline again` | 27 | 11 (41%) | +5% | 0.549 |
| `kit: clean again` | 27 | 0 (0%) | +0% | 1.000 |

In `lab: baseline again`, 11 of 27 cases (41%) separated two sides with nothing between them to separate. That is the price of calling a case informative on one difference alone, and the reason the pruning experiment below keeps a case only when some other comparison — never the one being recomputed — found it informative. The same procedure on `kit: clean again` flagged nothing at all. What differs between those two answers is not the criterion, which is identical, but the suite it was applied to: the one that disagrees with its own repeats hands any criterion something to find.

## 8. Would the conclusions survive a shorter suite?

Each comparison recomputed on the cases that *other* comparisons found informative — never on the ones it decided itself.

| Comparison | Kept | Dropped | Δ all | Δ kept | p all | p kept | Time saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lab: improved [scorer=1.1.0 time_limit=120]` | 19 | 8 | +15% | +26% | 0.302 | 0.092 | 18% |
| `lab: improved [scorer=1.2.0 time_limit=120]` | 18 | 9 | +12% | +18% | 0.332 | 0.424 | 31% |
| `lab: improved [scorer=1.2.0 time_limit=300]` | 20 | 7 | +21% | +27% | 0.003 | 0.006 | 19% |
| `kit: untrusted-content-unmarked [harness=v1]` | 11 | 16 | +0% | -9% | 1.000 | 1.000 | 36% |
| `kit: untrusted-content-unmarked [harness=v2]` | 11 | 16 | +0% | -9% | 1.000 | 1.000 | 55% |
| `kit: vague-tool-descriptions [harness=v1]` | 12 | 15 | -22% | -50% | 0.070 | 0.070 | 35% |
| `kit: vague-tool-descriptions [harness=v2]` | 10 | 17 | -15% | -40% | 0.289 | 0.219 | 41% |

Same verdict at 5% in 7 of 7 comparisons, with 18% to 55% of the running time removed. The effects get larger on the shorter suite, because the silent cases were averaging them down.

Read the `lab` rows against the A/A pair above: on that suite 11 of 27 cases separate two runs of one variant, so some of what is kept here was selected by noise rather than by the policy. The verdicts survive either way — that is what the p columns say — but the set of cases doing the surviving is softer than a count of them suggests.

## What this does not show

The corpus is two suites written by one person, scored by two small local models over eleven
days. Everything here describes those suites: a saturated case is saturated *for these
models*, and a case that never moved might move against a better one. The same measurements
on a suite with more models and more runs would be worth more, and the queries would not
change.

Nothing here judges the models. A suite that cannot separate two policies is a statement
about the suite; which policy is better is a question these runs already answered elsewhere.

The A/A pairs are one repeat each. Two runs of one variant give a point estimate of how much
a suite moves on its own, not an interval around it, and the honest reading of the assistant
suite's eleven cases is "many", not "eleven".
